Risk rating policy

Likelihood and impact use an ordinal 1–5 scale. Their product helps prioritize scenarios: Low 1–5, Moderate 6–11, High 12–19, Critical 20–25. This is this workspace’s policy, not a prescribed NIST scoring formula or an EU classification. Do not average risks across a system.

Likelihood over the next review period

  1. Exceptional: credible but unusual in this context.
  2. Unlikely: possible with limited exposure.
  3. Plausible: realistic under expected use.
  4. Likely: repeated opportunities or observed failures.
  5. Frequent: expected repeatedly without intervention.

Impact on people and the organization

  1. Minor inconvenience, readily corrected.
  2. Limited harm or rework with straightforward recovery.
  3. Material harm, service disruption or significant disadvantage.
  4. Serious safety, privacy, rights or business consequences.
  5. Severe or irreversible harm, at scale or to vulnerable people.

Use the highest credible impact dimension and explain exposure, affected groups and uncertainty. Missing answers remain Unassessed. Planned controls cannot justify a lower residual rating. Each applicable risk needs current, independently verified evidence before approval.

Critical residual risks block approval. High residual risks require a conditional decision and an action with an owner and future due date. The assessment author cannot approve their own work.

EU applicability and timing

Screen the actual intended use and organization’s role. An organization adopting a vendor system may also acquire provider duties if it changes or rebrands the system in relevant circumstances. Article 25.

Potential high-risk uses need a documented Article 6 and Annex I/III review. An exception cannot be inferred simply because a human is involved. Article 6.

For applicable high-risk systems, plan deployer oversight, monitoring, controlled logs and incident handling. Fundamental rights impact assessments are required for specified categories of deployers and uses, not universally. Article 26 · Article 27.

The amended timetable sets the main Annex III high-risk duties for 2 December 2027 and Annex I high-risk duties for 2 August 2028. Article 50 generally applies from 2 August 2026, with a targeted Article 50(2) transition for certain existing systems to 2 December 2026. Specific transitions and exceptions must be reviewed. Commission timetable · 2026 amendment.

Article 50 distinguishes provider duties from deployer duties and contains specific exceptions. Review the applicable paragraph instead of treating every chatbot or generated text alike. Article 50.

This workspace records assessment and review evidence. Its screening does not establish legal compliance or replace a qualified assessment of applicability.
